AUDIO PRODUCTION, VOICE-OVER & SOUND DESIGN

1. Core Philosophy

Audio is not an accessory to the video.

Voice, music, sound effects, ambience, and silence work together to create the audience's experience.

A strong visual without appropriate audio may feel lifeless.

A strong voice-over without visual support may feel like a podcast with illustrations.

The objective is to create a coherent audiovisual experience.

Voice communicates the story. Sound creates the atmosphere. Visuals make the story tangible.

---

2. Audio Production Components

The agent must understand five major components.

Component| Purpose
Voice-Over| Deliver narration, arguments, and storytelling
Background Music| Establish emotional atmosphere and pacing
Sound Effects| Reinforce actions and visual events
Ambient Sound| Establish environmental context
Silence| Create emphasis, anticipation, or reflection

Each component must have a clear purpose.

Do not add audio simply because a timeline contains empty space.

---

3. Voice-Over Direction

Voice-over is not just reading the script.

The agent must direct how the script should sound.

Before generating voice-over, define a voice direction profile.

Voice Profile

Specify:

- Language.
- Accent or regional pronunciation.
- Voice gender, if required.
- Voice character.
- Vocal age impression.
- Pitch.
- Timbre.
- Speaking pace.
- Articulation.
- Emotional character.
- Narration style.

Recommended Voice Identity

For this channel:

- Natural Indonesian.
- Conversational delivery.
- Clear articulation.
- Mature and credible presence.
- Expressive but controlled.
- Human-sounding delivery.
- Not excessively theatrical.
- Not like a formal news presenter.
- Not like an advertisement.
- Not like a motivational speech.

The voice should feel like someone explaining an interesting observation to another person.

It should not sound like someone reading an essay.

---

4. Voice Direction by Content Category

Different subjects require different delivery.

Relationship Commentary

Recommended delivery:

- Empathetic.
- Reflective.
- Intimate.
- Emotionally aware.
- Controlled pacing.

Avoid:

- Excessive sadness.
- Artificial romantic drama.
- Overacting.

Political Commentary

Recommended delivery:

- Clear.
- Confident.
- Analytical.
- Firm when presenting criticism.
- Controlled emotional intensity.

Avoid:

- Aggressive shouting.
- Fake outrage.
- Sensationalist delivery.
- Presenting speculation as established fact.

Viral Social Phenomena

Recommended delivery:

- Curious.
- Conversational.
- Engaging.
- Expressive.
- Flexible pacing.

Avoid:

- Constant excitement.
- Artificial surprise.
- Exaggerated reactions.

5. Script Adaptation for Voice

A script written for reading is not automatically suitable for speaking.

Before voice generation, perform a spoken-language review.

Check:

- Sentence length.
- Natural breathing points.
- Pronunciation difficulty.
- Unnatural punctuation.
- Repetitive sentence structures.
- Difficult names and terminology.
- Ambiguous abbreviations.
- Rhythm and emphasis.

Example

Written version:

"Fenomena tersebut menunjukkan adanya kecenderungan masyarakat dalam membentuk penilaian berdasarkan informasi yang tidak utuh."

Spoken version:

"Fenomena ini memperlihatkan sesuatu. Orang bisa membentuk penilaian, bahkan ketika informasi yang mereka punya sebenarnya belum lengkap."

The spoken version is easier to deliver naturally.

Do not change the factual meaning when adapting a script.

Voice-Over Script Markers

Use clear production markers when supported by the voice tool.

Examples:

- [PAUSE]
- [SHORT PAUSE]
- [EMPHASIS]
- [SLOWER]
- [LOWER INTENSITY]
- [ENERGY INCREASE]

These are direction markers, not necessarily words to be spoken.

The agent must verify whether its TTS tool supports such controls. If not, use supported SSML, sentence segmentation, or audio editing methods.

Never assume bracketed instructions will automatically affect a voice generator.

6. Voice Generation and Tool Utilization

Before generating voice-over:

1. Inspect available voice generation tools.
2. Verify language support.
3. Verify Indonesian pronunciation quality.
4. Identify available voices.
5. Check speed and pitch controls.
6. Check emotional or expressive controls.
7. Check supported audio formats.
8. Check maximum generation duration.
9. Check whether generation can be performed in segments.
10. Test a short sample before generating the full narration.

Classify each capability:

- VERIFIED.
- AVAILABLE.
- LIMITED.
- UNAVAILABLE.
- UNKNOWN.

Voice Generation Workflow

Step 1 — Prepare the Script

Complete the spoken-language review.

Step 2 — Select Voice

Choose a voice consistent with the channel identity.

Step 3 — Create a Test

Generate a short paragraph containing:

- A question.
- A normal statement.
- A critical statement.
- An emotional or reflective sentence.

Step 4 — Evaluate

Listen for:

- Naturalness.
- Pronunciation.
- Intonation.
- Rhythm.
- Emotional expression.
- Consistency.
- Unwanted robotic delivery.

Step 5 — Generate Full Narration

Generate in manageable segments when necessary.

Step 6 — Review the Actual Audio

Never assume generated audio is correct merely because the tool returned a successful response.

7. Voice Consistency

Recurring videos should maintain a recognizable voice identity.

Create a voice profile record containing:

- Voice identifier.
- Provider or tool.
- Voice settings.
- Language and accent.
- Speed.
- Pitch.
- Style instructions.
- Output format.
- Reference sample.
- Known limitations.

Avoid changing voices randomly between videos.

If a voice provider becomes unavailable, test a replacement before adopting it as the new channel voice.

Do not assume two different voice models will sound identical even with similar settings.

---

8. Audio Editing and Processing

After voice generation, inspect and prepare the audio.

Potential processing:

- Trimming.
- Noise reduction.
- Equalization.
- Compression.
- De-essing.
- Loudness adjustment.
- Fades.
- Segment alignment.
- Breath and pause management.

Important Principles

Noise Reduction

Remove unwanted noise without damaging voice quality.

Equalization

Improve clarity and reduce unnecessary frequency buildup.

Compression

Control excessive differences between loud and quiet sections.

De-Essing

Reduce harsh sibilance when necessary.

Loudness

Maintain consistent perceived volume.

Do not apply aggressive processing simply because a professional production workflow includes it.

Processing must improve the actual recording.

9. Background Music Direction

Music must support the emotional direction of the story.

Choose music according to:

- Subject.
- Narrative progression.
- Emotional intensity.
- Voice characteristics.
- Visual rhythm.
- Audience expectations.

Music Direction by Category

Content| Possible Music Direction
Relationship| Minimal, emotional, atmospheric
Politics| Subtle, serious, investigative
Viral phenomena| Contemporary, rhythmic, curious
Reflection| Sparse, calm, atmospheric
Revelation| Controlled build-up or restrained impact

Avoid:

- Constantly dramatic music.
- Music louder than narration.
- Generic cinematic music in every scene.
- Emotional music that manipulates the interpretation.
- Repetitive musical transitions.

Music must support the editorial intention, not replace it.

---

10. Sound Effects and Ambience

Sound effects should correspond to visible events.

Examples:

Visual Event| Possible Audio
Phone notification| Notification sound
Message appears| Subtle interface sound
Object falls| Impact sound
Scene reveal| Controlled transition effect
Busy city| Urban ambience
Empty room| Room tone or subtle ambience
Information spreads| Layered notification sounds
Important realization| Music reduction or brief silence

Do not use sound effects randomly.

A sound effect must have a visual or narrative reason.

Audio Restraint

Not every movement needs a sound.

Not every transition needs a whoosh.

Not every emotional moment needs music.

Silence is a valid production decision.

---

11. Audio and Visual Synchronization

Voice-over should determine the primary narration timeline.

Visuals should be synchronized with meaningful spoken moments.

The agent must identify:

- Important words.
- Important claims.
- Emotional emphasis.
- Narrative reveals.
- Pauses.
- Sentence transitions.
- Scene changes.

Example

Voice-over:

"Awalnya hanya satu komentar."

Visual:
One comment appears.

Voice-over:

"Tapi kemudian, orang lain mulai ikut menanggapi."

Visual:
Additional comments appear.

Voice-over:

"Dan konflik itu berkembang jauh melampaui masalah awalnya."

Visual:
The composition expands into multiple conversations and environments.

The audio and visual sequence develop together.

Synchronization Rules

- Do not cut randomly in the middle of important visual actions.
- Avoid showing key information before the narration establishes its context.
- Use pauses to allow important visuals to register.
- Do not force every word to have a matching animation.
- Preserve natural narration rhythm.

---

12. Audio Mixing Hierarchy

The audience must understand the narration.

A general priority:

1. Voice-over clarity.
2. Important sound effects.
3. Background music.
4. Ambient sound.

This is a functional hierarchy, not a fixed loudness formula.

Use appropriate gain, compression, ducking, and automation.

Music and ambience should not compete with speech frequencies or obscure important words.

Measure final loudness using the actual platform and production requirements.

Do not assume one universal loudness target is optimal for every platform or format.

13. Practical Exercise

Topic:

"Kenapa orang lebih mudah percaya pada informasi yang sesuai dengan keyakinannya?"

Create a 30–45 second audiovisual sequence.

Requirements:

- One consistent Indonesian voice identity.
- Natural conversational narration.
- At least three different delivery directions.
- Background music appropriate to the topic.
- At least three purposeful sound effects.
- One moment using silence or reduced audio.
- Visual synchronization with important narration moments.
- Clean audio with understandable speech.
- No excessive sound effects.

Deliver:

1. Voice profile.
2. Spoken-language script.
3. Voice-generation settings.
4. Generated audio.
5. Audio edit plan.
6. Music and sound-effect plan.
7. Synchronized video sample.

14. Evaluation

Score each criterion from 0 to 5.

Criterion| Evaluation
Voice Naturalness| Does the narration sound human and conversational?
Pronunciation| Is Indonesian pronunciation clear and accurate?
Voice Direction| Does delivery match the content?
Audio Quality| Is the recording clean and consistent?
Music Selection| Does music support the narrative?
Sound Design| Are effects meaningful and controlled?
Synchronization| Do audio and visuals work together?

Passing score: 26/35.

Critical failure:

If the voice-over sounds like an unedited robotic reading, or music and effects consistently obscure narration, the production cannot pass.

---

15. Definition of Done

The agent has mastered this knowledge when it can:

- Prepare scripts for spoken delivery.
- Define a consistent voice identity.
- Inspect and use available voice-generation tools.
- Generate Indonesian voice-over.
- Evaluate actual generated audio.
- Direct different emotional deliveries.
- Edit and process audio.
- Select music according to narrative purpose.
- Use sound effects and ambience appropriately.
- Synchronize voice-over with visuals.
- Produce a balanced final audio mix.

Final Principle:

Do not simply make the script audible.

Make the audience feel that someone is telling them a meaningful story.
