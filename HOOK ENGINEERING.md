HOOK ENGINEERING

Knowledge Base — Script Intelligence

1. PURPOSE

Teach the agent to create compelling video openings that capture attention through curiosity, relevance, tension, and human psychology.

A hook is not merely an attractive opening sentence.

It is the beginning of a psychological agreement between the content and the audience.

The audience gives attention because they expect something interesting, meaningful, or valuable in return.

The content must fulfill that expectation.

2. THE PSYCHOLOGY OF A HOOK

An effective hook generally activates one or more of these responses:

A. CURIOSITY

The audience recognizes an unanswered question.

Example:

"Kenapa orang yang paling sering bilang dirinya tidak peduli justru terkadang paling sibuk memperhatikan kehidupan orang lain?"

B. CONTRADICTION

The opening challenges an assumption or reveals an inconsistency.

Example:

"Kita sering bilang ingin kejujuran. Tapi ketika seseorang benar-benar jujur, belum tentu kita siap mendengarnya."

C. RECOGNITION

The audience recognizes something familiar in their own lives.

Example:

"Pernah melihat seseorang yang selalu membela temannya, bahkan ketika jelas-jelas temannya melakukan kesalahan?"

D. STAKES

The opening establishes why the subject matters.

Example:

"Ketika sebuah kebijakan dibuat tanpa transparansi, dampaknya tidak berhenti di ruang pemerintahan. Masyarakat yang menanggung konsekuensinya."

E. SURPRISE

The audience encounters an unexpected connection or observation.

Example:

"Kadang, yang membuat sebuah hubungan bertahan bukan karena dua orang saling memahami, tetapi karena salah satunya terlalu takut kehilangan."

These are creative approaches, not guaranteed psychological triggers.

Choose them according to the actual story.

3. HOOK ARCHETYPES

The agent should understand several hook patterns.

1. THE UNCOMFORTABLE QUESTION

Ask something the audience may have experienced but rarely examines.

Example:
"Kalau seseorang terus-menerus menyakiti kita, kenapa justru kita yang merasa bersalah ketika memilih pergi?"

Best for:

- Relationships.
- Social behavior.
- Human psychology.

2. THE CONTRADICTION

Expose a gap between what people say and what they do.

Example:
"Kita ingin pemimpin yang transparan. Tapi seberapa sering kita benar-benar memeriksa informasi sebelum membela mereka?"

Best for:

- Politics.
- Public behavior.
- Social commentary.

3. THE UNEXPECTED OBSERVATION

Present an observation that changes how the audience sees a familiar situation.

Example:
"Di internet, orang tidak selalu mencari kebenaran. Terkadang mereka hanya mencari alasan untuk merasa bahwa dirinya benar."

Best for:

- Viral phenomena.
- Social media behavior.
- Public controversies.

4. THE SCENE-BASED OPENING

Start with a recognizable human situation.

Example:
"Bayangkan kamu baru saja mengunggah satu foto. Beberapa menit kemudian, ribuan orang mulai menilai hidupmu dari satu gambar itu."

Best for:

- Emotional storytelling.
- Relatable situations.
- Narrative-driven videos.

5. THE IRONIC STATEMENT

Use an unexpected contrast to create interest.

Example:
"Kita hidup di zaman ketika semua orang bisa berbicara, tetapi mendengarkan justru semakin sulit."

Best for:

- Cultural commentary.
- Social criticism.
- Everyday contradictions.

6. THE FACTUAL REVELATION

Open with a verified fact that changes the context of a story.

Example:
Begin with a documented timeline or statistic that contradicts the common understanding of an event.

Best for:

- Political developments.
- Investigative commentary.
- Current affairs.

Requirements:

- Verify the source.
- Preserve the original context.
- Never exaggerate the significance of a statistic.
- Attribute disputed claims appropriately.

7. THE CONFLICT OPENING

Introduce two opposing positions or interests.

Example:
"Di satu sisi, masyarakat menuntut harga yang lebih terjangkau. Di sisi lain, kebijakan yang dibuat justru memunculkan pertanyaan tentang siapa yang sebenarnya diuntungkan."

Best for:

- Public policy.
- Social conflicts.
- Political commentary.

4. THE HOOK PROMISE

Every hook creates an expectation.

The next part of the video must fulfill that expectation.

A common failure is opening with a dramatic statement and then delivering unrelated background information.

Example:

HOOK:
"Kenapa seseorang bisa tetap bertahan dalam hubungan yang jelas-jelas menyakitinya?"

BAD CONTINUATION:
"Hubungan manusia merupakan salah satu aspek penting dalam kehidupan sosial."

BETTER CONTINUATION:
"Jawabannya tidak selalu sesederhana masih cinta. Ada ketakutan kehilangan, kebiasaan, harapan bahwa pasangan akan berubah, dan berbagai faktor lain yang bisa membuat seseorang sulit pergi."

The continuation should immediately advance the question established by the opening.

---

5. THE FIRST 10 SECONDS

The first 10 seconds should establish why the video deserves attention.

A useful progression:

- 0–3 seconds: Introduce a striking observation, question, or situation.
- 3–6 seconds: Develop the tension or contradiction.
- 6–10 seconds: Establish the direction of the story.

These timings are guidelines, not rigid rules.

The actual pacing depends on narration speed, visual complexity, platform, and video format.

Avoid:

- Long greetings.
- Channel introductions.
- Generic news summaries.
- Unnecessary disclaimers at the beginning.
- Repeating the title in spoken form.
- Asking viewers to like and subscribe before delivering value.

6. COMMON HOOK FAILURES

Failure A — Generic opening

"Belakangan ini, masyarakat sedang dihebohkan oleh..."

Problem:
The audience already knows that something is trending. The sentence offers no distinctive perspective.

Failure B — Empty clickbait

"Fakta mengejutkan yang tidak pernah kamu ketahui!"

Problem:
It promises importance without explaining why the subject deserves attention.

Failure C — Excessive drama

"Indonesia sedang berada di ambang kehancuran!"

Problem:
An exaggerated statement can damage credibility when the evidence does not support it.

Failure D — Artificial philosophical language

"Di balik setiap realitas, terdapat sebuah paradoks kehidupan yang sering kali terlupakan."

Problem:
The sentence sounds profound but communicates very little.

Failure E — Unnecessary rhetorical questions

Using multiple questions consecutively without developing any of them.

Problem:
The audience experiences repetition rather than progression.

Failure F — Unsupported accusations

Using a provocative hook to imply wrongdoing by a real person without sufficient evidence.

Problem:
Attention is gained at the expense of editorial integrity.

7. HOOK GENERATION WORKFLOW

Before writing the opening, the agent must:

1. Understand the subject.
2. Identify the most interesting contradiction or observation.
3. Determine the audience's likely familiarity with the topic.
4. Identify what remains unanswered.
5. Select an appropriate hook archetype.
6. Write multiple genuinely different openings.
7. Check whether the script fulfills each opening's promise.

Do not generate multiple hooks by simply changing a few words.

Each alternative should represent a different narrative direction.

---

8. PRACTICAL TRAINING EXERCISES

EXERCISE A — Hook Analysis

Provide the agent with five real video openings.

Ask it to identify:

- Hook archetype.
- Central curiosity.
- Emotional tone.
- Promise made to the audience.
- How the continuation fulfills that promise.

The agent must explain its reasoning.

EXERCISE B — Multiple Perspectives

Input one topic.

Generate five distinct openings using five different archetypes.

The openings must not repeat the same central idea.

EXERCISE C — Hook and Continuation

For each opening, write the next 10–20 seconds of narration.

Evaluate whether the continuation naturally fulfills the opening's promise.

EXERCISE D — Anti-Clickbait Test

Ask the agent to identify:

- Exaggerated claims.
- Unsupported implications.
- Empty curiosity.
- Artificial suspense.
- Misleading framing.

Revise every opening that fails.

9. SELF-EVALUATION

Score each criterion from 0–5.

Criterion| Score
Immediate relevance| 0–5
Curiosity or tension| 0–5
Natural language| 0–5
Distinctiveness| 0–5
Promise clarity| 0–5
Continuation potential| 0–5
Factual integrity| 0–5

Maximum: 35 points.

Minimum passing score: 27 points.

Automatic revision required if:

- The opening misrepresents the story.
- The hook makes an unsupported factual implication.
- The continuation fails to fulfill its promise.
- The language is generic or unnatural.
- The hook depends entirely on exaggerated drama.

A high score does not guarantee high audience retention. Actual performance must eventually be evaluated using published video analytics.

---

10. COMPLETION STANDARD

The agent must demonstrate that it can:

- Identify the central curiosity of a topic.
- Select appropriate hook archetypes.
- Produce genuinely different opening concepts.
- Write natural Indonesian openings.
- Connect the hook to the next section.
- Avoid empty clickbait and unsupported claims.
- Explain why one opening is more suitable than another.

Do not proceed to advanced narrative tension until these abilities are demonstrated consistently.
